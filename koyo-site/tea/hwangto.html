THE STORY — 이야기

Hwangto (황토) is a yellow-red clay that Koreans call living soil. Said to breathe with the earth, purifying the air while harmonizing the force that brings life, it formed the walls and floors of traditional homes — hanok (한옥) — standing as a quiet, enduring cornerstone of Korean culture. We honor that foundation with a blend that unites Korean-style red tea, hongcha (홍차), and yellow quince fruit, mogwa (모과).

Our red tea is made from the same Hadong plants as our Sejak, fully oxidized in the manner of black tea — but it brews to a softer crimson, with less caffeine and a sweeter profile, known for gently insulating the drinker from within. The craft behind it draws on both Korean and Taiwanese techniques, refined in small organic batches and hand-picked so those subtle differences stay in the cup.

The quince carries its own legacy: traditionally used to soothe sore throats and dry coughs, a fruit of respiratory healing. Though no longer central to daily life, quince trees still grace Korean courtyards as quiet symbols of strength and purity — much like hwangto itself: ever-present, often unnoticed, deeply rooted.

HOW TO BREW — 손맛

Brewed by feel.

  1. 一

    Boil

    Bring mineral water to a full boil — the blend opens at 100°C.

  2. 二

    Steep

    Three grams to 210 ml of water. Two minutes, and the crimson arrives.

  3. 三

    Return

    Steep once more. The quince sweetens as the red tea softens.

These are recommendations, never law — sohn-mat trusts your hands.
